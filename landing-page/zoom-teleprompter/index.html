Why Zoom calls need a teleprompter overlay

Zoom's gallery view pushes faces to the top third of your screen. Your webcam sits above the display. Your notes sit on your desk or in a window below your camera. That vertical distance is the problem:

  • Looking down at notes reads as disengaged or unprepared
  • Side-glancing at a second monitor is obvious to everyone
  • Reading from a script in your lap makes your eyes move erratically

Glance places your script in the top zone of your screen — right next to your camera — so you can read without breaking eye contact.

Setting up Glance with Zoom

The ideal Zoom + Glance layout takes 30 seconds:

  1. Open Zoom and enter your meeting or start a test call
  2. Launch Glance — it appears as a transparent overlay near your camera
  3. Drop your script — paste text or import a Markdown file
  4. Position Glance in the top-center of your screen, just below your webcam
  5. Adjust opacity so your script is readable but your video feed is visible behind it

Glance stays on top of Zoom without hiding behind it. Your audience sees only your face — not the overlay.

Tips for screen sharing on Zoom

When you share your screen on Zoom, participants see whatever is on your display. If you share your entire screen, Glance will be visible. If you share a single window or application, Glance stays hidden.

Best practice for demos:

  • Share only the Zoom window, not the full screen
  • Or share a browser tab for slides or product walkthroughs
  • Glance stays private on your main display while your audience sees only the shared window

Test your specific setup before a live call — every monitor arrangement is slightly different.

Built for live calls, not recording

Most teleprompter apps are designed for video production — you write a script, record a take, and edit it later. Glance is designed for the moment that matters: the live Zoom call where you cannot redo your lines.

You get real-time controls that matter in conversation:

  • Auto-Pause with Voice — Glance pauses when you stop speaking, resumes when you continue
  • Speed adjustment — slow down on complex points, speed through transitions
  • Section jumps — use Markdown headings + keyboard shortcuts to skip between topics
  • Quick Hide — instantly dismiss the overlay when you go off-script

Privacy-first by design

Glance runs entirely on your device. Your scripts are never uploaded, and Glance has no recording capabilities. Audio analysis for Auto-Pause happens locally — nothing is stored or transmitted.

  • No accounts. No login.
  • Offline after activation. Use the internet once to activate your license, then keep working offline.
  • No recording. Glance does not record your screen, camera, or audio.
  • Your scripts stay on your device. Always.

FAQ

Does Glance appear in my Zoom camera feed?

No. Glance is an on-screen overlay on your device. It never appears in your camera feed.

Will my meeting participants see the teleprompter if I share my screen?

Only if you share your entire screen. If you share a single window (like a browser) or a specific application, Glance stays invisible to participants.

Does Glance work with Zoom on both macOS and Windows?

Yes. Glance is available for macOS and Windows. The overlay behaviour is identical on both platforms.

Can I use Glance with Zoom gallery view?

Yes. Glance sits on top of your Zoom window regardless of view mode — gallery, speaker, or fullscreen.

Does Glance work with Zoom breakout rooms?

Yes. Glance stays active across all Zoom contexts — main room, breakout rooms, and waiting rooms.

How do I prevent Glance from showing during a Zoom recording?

Use Quick Hide (keyboard shortcut) before starting the recording, or share a single window instead of your full screen.

Do I need an account?

No account required. Glance is a standalone app with no login.

Can I use Glance offline?

Yes. After a one-time internet connection to activate your license on this device, Glance can keep working offline.
