How to Present on Google Meet with Notes (Without Looking Away)

A step-by-step guide to presenting Google Slides or pitch decks on Google Meet on a single monitor while keeping your private notes anchored right below your camera.

Presenting on Google Meet presents a notorious single-monitor dilemma. If you click Present on Google Slides, full screen takes over your entire display—cutting off your view of attendee video tiles, the chat window, and your speaking notes.

If you open Google Slides' built-in Presenter View, it launches a separate floating popup window. To read your speaker notes, your eyes must constantly drift down to the corner of your screen or off to a secondary monitor. To everyone on the Google Meet call, it looks like you are distracted, unconfident, or reading off a cheat sheet.

Here is how to set up an ergonomic, professional presenter layout on Google Meet so your notes stay completely private, floating directly below your webcam lens, while keeping full visibility of your audience.

The Core Rule: Share a Chrome Tab or Window, Never "Entire Screen"

When you click Present now in Google Meet, you are offered three distinct capture methods:

  • A Chrome tab (Best for video & animation): Captures only the DOM elements inside a specific browser tab. It delivers the highest frame rate (up to 60fps) and cleanest audio pass-through.
  • A Window: Captures everything inside a single chosen application window (e.g. Chrome, PowerPoint, or Keynote).
  • Entire Screen: Broadcasts every single pixel on that monitor, including system notifications, background app windows, and private messages.

Privacy Guarantee: When you share A Chrome tab or A Window, native desktop overlays like Glance remain completely invisible to everyone on Google Meet, even when positioned directly over your slides.

Step-by-Step Setup Guide for Google Slides

1. Prepare Your Script or Talking Points in Glance

Load your script, bullet points, or customer data points into Glance. Adjust font size and opacity so text is effortless to read without squinting. Keep each bullet short and conversational.

2. Anchor Your Teleprompter Directly Below Your Webcam

Drag the Glance overlay to the top center of your display, sitting directly below your physical camera or built-in laptop lens. By placing your notes within 1–2 inches of the camera aperture, reading your script creates the natural illusion of looking directly into your colleagues' eyes.

3. Present Google Slides Without Losing Your Window Layout

Instead of clicking the default full-screen Slideshow button in Google Slides:

  1. Click the small down-arrow next to Slideshow.
  2. Select Presenter view (or use keyboard shortcut Cmd + Shift + Return on Mac, Ctrl + Shift + Enter on Windows).
  3. Minimize or close the separate notes popup window—you won't need it because Glance is already handling your notes right beside your camera.
  4. Keep the presentation tab inside your standard browser window.

4. Start the Share in Google Meet

In Google Meet, click Present now → A Chrome tab, and select your Google Slides tab. A blue sharing banner will appear at the top of the tab confirming that only this tab is being broadcast.

5. Keep Attendee Reactions in View

Position your Google Meet meeting tab or window directly beneath your Glance overlay. This lets you read your talking points while simultaneously noticing head nods, confused glances, or hands raised in the meeting room.
